Gas-fired tankless water heaters use a burner to directly heat water as it flows through the unit. Electric tankless water heaters heat water using electrical resistance. Common Problems We Help Solve

Not Enough Space for a Water Heater

Unless a property has a large dedicated storage area, conventional tank water heaters can occupy an excessive amount of space. A tankless water heater installation can make use of a smaller footprint, although it has its own clearances and mounting requirements.

Ran Out of Hot Water

A tank water heater can lose all of its available hot water at once if the occupants' demand exceeds the unit's capability. A properly sized tankless unit can supply an endless stream of hot water within its limitations, and should be chosen based on the occupants' simultaneous demand. If the household has no hot water, a no hot water plumber Huntington Beach search can help homeowners find service options to assess whether repair or replacement is appropriate.

Concerns about Energy Efficiency

A traditional tank water heater loses some of the heat from the water to the environment, requiring extra energy to maintain its temperature. A tankless water heater can reduce this loss, but the efficiency depends on several factors unique to each property.

Old or Failing Water Heater

If a property owner has concerns about their tank water heater, there may be a reason to consider switching to a tankless unit. However, costs and requirements should be taken into account when switching between fuel types or making plumbing changes.
